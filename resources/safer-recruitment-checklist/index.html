The safer recruitment checklist.

Every member of staff in a children’s home needs a recruitment file that shows they are fit to work there. Ofsted will read these files. This is what goes in each one, in plain words, in the order you collect it.

Before the offer

Application form, not just a CV

A CV alone is not enough. The form must ask for a full work history and for permission to run checks.

Full employment history, with every gap explained

Every job since leaving school, with dates. Any gap of a month or more needs a written reason, kept on file next to the history.

Interview record

Who interviewed, when, what was asked about working with children, and why the person was chosen. Keep the notes.

Checks before the start date

Two references, verified

One must be from the most recent employer. If they worked with children before, ask that employer why they left. Phone to confirm each reference is real, and note the call.

Enhanced DBS, child workforce, with barred-list check

The right level for a children’s home. Record the certificate number and date. Decide your renewal cycle and write it down.

Right to work in the UK

Checked and dated, with a copy of what you saw.

Identity and qualifications

Passport or driving licence copied. Certificates for any qualification the role relies on, seen and copied.

Overseas checks, if they lived abroad

A police check or letter of good conduct from each country they lived in as an adult.

Health declaration

A statement that the person is fit for the role, kept on file.

The gaps inspections find most often

A reference that was never verified by phone. A work-history gap with no written reason. A DBS with no renewal date, so nobody noticed it getting old. An interview with no notes. None of these are hard to fix. All of them are hard to fix on the day.
